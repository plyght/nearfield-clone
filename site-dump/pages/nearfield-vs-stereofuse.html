The free route

Free—but without global volume control.

Audio MIDI Setup can pair the displays, but selecting the aggregate disables macOS’s global volume controls—an impractical trade-off for daily use.

  1. 01
    Create an Aggregate Device

    Open Audio MIDI Setup, click the + button, choose Create Aggregate Device, and tick both Studio Displays.

  2. 02
    Assign left and right

    Open Configure Speakers, choose Stereo, and map each display to its side of the desk.

  3. 03
    Select it as your output

    Pick the new device in Sound settings so every app plays through both displays.

  4. 04
    Maintain it

    Re-check the setup when a display reconnects, and adjust loudness with per-device sliders—the keyboard and menu bar volume controls are disabled.

What you give up

  • Global volume control, including keyboard keys
  • Automatic reconnect recovery
  • Left/right test tones and one-control swap
  • Per-display balance

Nearfield performs this whole setup at install, keeps it healthy while displays come and go, and removes everything on uninstall.

Nearfield vs StereoFuse

No microphone access. More control.

Common questions

Before you choose.

What is the simplest way to use two Studio Displays as a stereo pair?+

A purpose-built app is the shortest route. Nearfield and StereoFuse both detect two Studio Displays, assign a left and right side, and create one system-wide output with keyboard volume control. Nearfield supports macOS 14 and later; StereoFuse currently requires macOS 26 or later.

Can Audio MIDI Setup turn two Studio Displays into true stereo?+

Yes, but it is a manual setup. An Aggregate Device can combine the displays, and Configure Speakers can assign the left and right channels. Selecting that aggregate as the output disables macOS global volume control, so the keyboard and menu bar volume controls are unavailable.

How is Nearfield different from StereoFuse?+

Both solve the same core problem, but Nearfield never requests microphone access and includes app-based routing. StereoFuse’s setup requires microphone access for its routing daemon. Nearfield also supports macOS 14 and later, while StereoFuse currently requires macOS 26 or later.

When does Loopback make more sense?+

Choose Loopback when you need a general-purpose audio routing studio: combining apps and microphones, creating multi-channel virtual devices, or building custom monitoring paths. For one task—pairing two Studio Displays—it offers far more control, setup, and cost than most people need.
